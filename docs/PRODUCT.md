# Nexus Support Lite

## Product Specification

**Status:** Discovery validated  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** July 31, 2026

## 1. Vision

Nexus Support Lite is a responsive web application for centralizing and tracking internal support incidents and requests. Its purpose is to prevent cases from being lost across fragmented channels, route them to the appropriate team, and allow each requester to know their status and current assignee.

The first version will be validated with several selected organizations. The monetization model will not be defined at this stage.

## 2. Problem

Incidents and requests arrive through different channels, resulting in:

- Loss of traceability.
- Lack of visibility into case status.
- Unclear ownership by a team or individual.
- Duplicate handling or unattended incidents.
- Difficulty measuring resolution times and support volume.

## 3. First-Version Objectives

- Centralize incident registration and tracking.
- Keep the status and current assignee of each incident visible.
- Route each case to the appropriate team through topics.
- Prevent two agents from handling the same incident simultaneously.
- Maintain an auditable history of all actions.
- Collect real-world data to establish a baseline for resolution times.
- Validate the product with multiple organizations under a multi-tenant model.

## 4. Success Signals

- Requesters can view the status and assignee of their incidents.
- No incident is left without a responsible team.
- The time between registration and closure decreases; the baseline and targets will be established after an initial period of use.
- Teams can understand the volume and distribution of handled incidents.

## 5. Product Scope

### 5.1 Platform

- Responsive web application for desktop and mobile devices.
- No native mobile applications in the first version.

### 5.2 Organizational Model

- Multi-tenant architecture with complete isolation between organizations.
- Platform-level multi-IdP support.
- Each organization uses a single identity provider.
- Different organizations may use different identity providers.

## 6. Actors and Permissions

The same person may hold multiple roles simultaneously.

### 6.1 Requester

- Create incidents.
- View their incidents, statuses, assignee, comments, and visible history.
- Add comments and permitted attachments.
- Freely accept or change the topic suggested by AI.
- Select or modify the priority when registering an incident.

### 6.2 Agent

- View only incidents associated with their topics.
- Take an available incident.
- Confirm or adjust its priority.
- Add comments and permitted attachments.
- Fully delegate an incident to another person.
- Transfer an incident to another topic.
- Close an incident when they are the current assignee, with a required resolution description.

### 6.3 Organization Administrator

- Manage users and roles within their organization.
- Create and manage topics.
- View all incidents and reports for their organization.
- Cannot access information belonging to other tenants.
- Cannot configure or change the IdP.

### 6.4 Nexus Global Administrator

- Create organizations.
- Register the first administrator for each organization.
- Configure and change the IdP for each organization.
- Manage only global, tenant, and identity-related settings.
- Cannot view incidents, comments, attachments, history, reports, or any other operational data belonging to organizations.

## 7. Identity and Access

1. The person enters their email address.
2. Nexus identifies the associated organizations and the corresponding IdP.
3. If the email belongs to multiple organizations, the person selects which one to access.
4. Nexus redirects the person to the selected organization's identity provider.
5. Any identity successfully authenticated by that IdP may access Nexus.
6. On first access, Nexus automatically creates the account with the **Requester** role.

Each organization is responsible for controlling who may authenticate through its IdP. Nexus trusts the identities validated by that provider.

## 8. Topics

Topics represent the categories used to route incidents. Each topic contains:

- Name.
- Description.
- Status: active or inactive.
- Responsible agents.

Rules:

- Only active topics may be used for new incidents.
- An inactive topic remains associated with existing incidents to preserve their history.
- A new incident becomes available in the shared queue of the agents responsible for its topic.
- All those agents receive an in-app notification in Nexus.

## 9. Incident Data

### 9.1 Creation Data

- Number or identifier generated by Nexus.
- Subject.
- Free-text description.
- Topic.
- Priority: **Low**, **Medium**, **High**, or **Critical**.
- Organization.
- Requester.
- Status.
- Creation date and time.
- Permitted attachments, when applicable.

### 9.2 Operational Data

- Current assignee, displayed with the label **Assigned to**.
- Assignment date and time.
- Chronological comments.
- Action history.
- Resolution applied, required for closure.
- Closure date and time.

## 10. Main Flow

1. The requester enters the subject and description.
2. AI analyzes that information and suggests a topic and priority.
3. The requester freely accepts or changes either suggestion without providing a justification.
4. The requester submits the incident.
5. Nexus creates it with the **New** status and places it in the topic queue.
6. All agents responsible for the topic receive a notification through the Nexus notification bell.
7. An agent explicitly selects **Take incident**.
8. Nexus assigns the case to that agent, changes its status to **In Progress**, and prevents another agent from taking it simultaneously.
9. The agent confirms or adjusts the priority.
10. The requester and agent communicate through chronological comments.
11. The current assignee enters a required resolution description and closes the incident.
12. Nexus changes the status to **Closed**, records the action, and notifies the requester within the application.

## 11. Statuses

The first-version workflow is:

**New → In Progress → Closed**

### New

- The incident belongs to a topic and therefore has a responsible team.
- It does not yet have an individual assignee.
- It is available for an agent assigned to the topic to take.

### In Progress

- The incident has an assignee.
- Only that person, or the person to whom it is delegated, is responsible for the case.

### Closed

- The current assignee resolved the incident.
- The incident contains a required description of the resolution applied.

The **Resolved** status, requester validation, and reopening are reserved for a later stage.

## 12. Concurrency, Delegation, and Transfer

### 12.1 Taking an Incident

- Opening or viewing an incident does not assign it.
- Assignment occurs only through **Take incident**.
- The operation must be atomic to prevent two agents from taking the same incident simultaneously.
- Other agents are not notified when someone takes it; they can see the current assignee under **Assigned to**.

### 12.2 Delegation

- The current assignee may delegate the incident to another person.
- Responsibility is transferred completely.
- The history records who delegated it, to whom, and when.
- The recipient receives an in-app notification.

### 12.3 Topic Transfer

- An agent may transfer the incident when its topic is incorrect.
- Changing the topic removes the individual assignment.
- The incident returns to the **New** status.
- It becomes available to the agents responsible for the new topic.
- All those agents receive an in-app notification.
- The history retains the previous topic, the new topic, the author, and the date and time of the change.

## 13. Artificial Intelligence

AI supports decision-making; it is not the final authority.

### 13.1 Functions

- Suggest a topic based on the subject and description.
- Suggest a priority based on the subject, description, and impact expressed in free text.
- Clearly present recommendations as suggestions.

### 13.2 Human Oversight

- The requester may freely accept or change the suggested topic.
- The requester may freely accept or change the suggested priority.
- The agent may adjust the priority while handling the incident.
- No justification is required when a person chooses an option different from the AI suggestion.

### 13.3 Graceful Degradation

If AI is unavailable:

- The incident can still be registered without interruption.
- The requester manually selects the topic and priority.
- The AI failure does not block the main workflow.

## 14. Comments and Attachments

### 14.1 Comments

- Communication takes place through chronological comments within the incident.
- Real-time chat will not be implemented.
- Comments record their author and date and time.
- A new comment generates an in-app notification for the relevant participants.

### 14.2 Attachments

- Attachments are permitted when creating an incident and in subsequent comments.
- They are limited to lightweight files and safe types, such as images and text files.
- The maximum size and exact list of formats will be defined before implementation.
- They are temporary and exist only during the ticket's lifecycle or retention period.
- They are deleted in accordance with the incident retention or deletion policy.
- They do not constitute a permanent document repository.

## 15. Notifications

First-version notifications are displayed only within Nexus through a notification bell. No email notifications will be sent.

Minimum events:

- Incident creation, for all agents responsible for the topic.
- New comments, for the relevant participants.
- Delegation, for the new assignee.
- Topic transfer, for the agents assigned to the new topic.
- Closure, for the requester.

Taking an incident does not generate a notification for the other agents responsible for the topic.

## 16. History and Audit Trail

Each entry must identify the action, its author, and the date and time. At a minimum, the history records:

- Incident creation.
- Status changes.
- Priority changes.
- Assignments.
- Delegations.
- Topic transfers.
- Comments.
- Closure and the recorded resolution.

The history must preserve traceability within the applicable retention policy.

## 17. Queues, Filters, and Views

Minimum filters:

- Date range.
- Status.
- Priority.

Visibility restrictions:

- A requester can access their own incidents.
- An agent can access incidents associated with their topics.
- An organization administrator can access all incidents within their tenant.
- No role can access the operational content of another tenant.

Search by incident number, subject, description, or free text is outside the scope of this version.

## 18. Lightweight Reports

The first version includes basic reports by:

- Status.
- Topic.
- Priority.
- Assigned agent.
- Period.

It also includes a KPI for incidents closed by each agent.

KPI rules:

- An incident counts only when the agent closes it with a recorded resolution.
- It is an informational metric for work volume and distribution.
- It must not be used as a standalone performance measure or to compare agents because it does not represent complexity or quality.

Advanced reporting is outside the scope of this version.

## 19. Essential Non-Functional Requirements

### 19.1 Multi-Tenant Isolation

- Users, roles, topics, incidents, comments, attachments, history, notifications, and reports must be isolated by organization.
- Every operation and query must be evaluated within the active tenant.
- The global administrator has no access to operational content.

### 19.2 Security

- Federated authentication through the IdP configured for each organization.
- Authorization based on role, topic membership, and tenant.
- Attachment type and size validation.
- Controls against malicious files and unauthorized attachment access.
- Auditable records of relevant actions.

### 19.3 Usability

- Incident registration must be brief and easy to understand.
- AI suggestions must be clear and editable.
- Status, priority, and **Assigned to** must be visible in the incident details.
- The interface must adapt to desktop and mobile devices.

### 19.4 Functional Availability

- A failure in the AI component must not prevent users from creating or handling incidents.
- Taking an incident must handle concurrency to prevent duplicate assignments.

## 20. Out of Scope for the First Version

- Monetization and commercial plans.
- Automatic intake from email, Teams, or other channels.
- Email notifications.
- Native mobile applications.
- Real-time chat.
- SLA management.
- Satisfaction surveys.
- Text search.
- Advanced reports.
- **Resolved** status.
- Requester validation of closure.
- Incident reopening.
- Use of attachments as permanent document storage.

## 21. High-Level Acceptance Criteria

1. A person authenticated through an organization's IdP can access Nexus and is automatically created as a requester on first access.
2. A requester can register an incident with a subject, description, topic, and priority.
3. AI can suggest a topic and priority without enforcing either suggestion.
4. If AI fails, the incident can be created manually.
5. A new incident appears only to agents responsible for its topic and generates in-app notifications for them.
6. Only one agent can take an available incident; the operation prevents simultaneous assignment.
7. When taken, the incident moves to **In Progress** and displays **Assigned to**.
8. Delegation fully transfers responsibility and is recorded in the history.
9. A topic transfer removes the assignment, returns the incident to **New**, and publishes it in the new topic's queue.
10. The requester and agent can add chronological comments and permitted attachments.
11. Only the current assignee can close the incident, and a resolution is required.
12. The history shows who performed each action and when.
13. Date-range, status, and priority filters work according to the user's permissions.
14. Reports display basic distributions and the number of incidents closed by each agent.
15. No user, organization administrator, or global administrator can access another tenant's operational content.

## 22. Decisions Pending Before Implementation

The following decisions do not change the functional scope but must be made during technical design:

- Identity providers supported initially.
- A secure method for resolving the tenant from an email address without revealing organizations to third parties.
- Retention policy for incidents, history, and attachments.
- Maximum attachment size and exact permitted formats.
- Strategy for scanning and blocking malicious files.
- Exact recipients of comment notifications based on context.
- Visual and textual guidance for appropriate priority selection.
- Administrative fields required to create a tenant.
- Technical model for maintaining data isolation.
- Initial observation period for establishing the closure-time baseline.

## 23. Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Agreed Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Notification fatigue among all agents | Notifications are limited to the Nexus notification bell; no emails are sent. |
| Broad access caused by automatic user creation | Each organization controls who can authenticate through its IdP and assumes responsibility for that access. |
| Incorrect AI classification | Suggestions are never mandatory; requesters and agents retain human control. |
| AI failure or unavailability | The manual workflow remains available and does not block incident creation. |
| Attachments used as a permanent repository | Attachments are restricted to lightweight, safe files and are subject to the ticket lifecycle or retention policy. |
| KPI interpreted as a performance evaluation | The KPI is explicitly informational and must not be used to compare agents without considering quality and complexity. |
| Two agents take the same case | **Take incident** must be atomic and enforce concurrency control. |
| Data exposure between organizations | Tenant isolation is mandatory across authentication, authorization, queries, and storage. |

## 24. Potential Future Evolution

Although not part of the first version, Discovery identified the following possible extensions:

- **Resolved** status, requester confirmation, and reopening.
- Automatic intake from email, Teams, or other channels.
- SLA management.
- Satisfaction surveys.
- Advanced reports.
- Text search.
- Additional configurable notifications.
